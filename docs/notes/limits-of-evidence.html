Measuring how a rare event can disappear

If two events each occur 10% of the time, independence gives a 1% chance of both occurring. Keeping both individual rates fixed, it takes a change of exactly 2% in total variation distance to bring the joint probability to zero.

Unequal event rates

Let the individual probabilities be α\alphaα and β\betaβ, with α+β≤1\alpha+\beta\le1α+β≤1. These rates and the requirement that the events never coincide determine the joint distribution of their two indicators. Its total variation distance from independence is 2αβ2\alpha\beta2αβ. Total variation is the largest difference in probability that two distributions assign to the same event.

The mutual information of the two indicators under this distribution is asymptotic to αβ\alpha\betaαβ as both rates tend to zero, using natural logarithms.
