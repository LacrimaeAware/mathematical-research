Which columns of Pascal’s triangle reach every remainder?

  • Number theory
  • Combinatorics
  • For each fixed prime, a density-one set of columns attains every remainder modulo every power of the prime. A quantitative bound covers several primes simultaneously.

Fix a column kkk in Pascal’s triangle and read its entries (nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​) as nnn increases. Which remainders occur when those entries are divided by a power of a prime?

Column k=7k=7k=7 shows why the power matters. Its entries take all three possible remainders modulo 333. Modulo 999, the only remainders are 0,1,3,6,80,1,3,6,80,1,3,6,8.

How common are columns that reach every remainder?

Fix any prime ppp. Among the first XXX columns, the proportion that attain every remainder modulo every power of ppp tends to one as XXX grows. The same column works at every precision, though different entries can supply different remainders.

Sun and Zhang established this property for explicit families of columns and asked which columns have it for a given prime. The density theorem shows how common the property is. A full classification of the exceptional columns remains open in general. Sun and Zhang, Binomial coefficients and the ring of p-adic integers

Several primes at once

Choose a fixed bbb with 0<b<1/250<b<1/250<b<1/25. For a proportion tending to one of the columns k≤Xk\le Xk≤X, the conclusion holds simultaneously for every prime p≤(log⁡X)bp\le(\log X)^bp≤(logX)b. Those columns attain every remainder modulo every integer whose prime factors lie in that range, with arbitrary exponents. Sun and Zhang’s conjecture about columns satisfying the condition at every prime up to kkk remains open.
