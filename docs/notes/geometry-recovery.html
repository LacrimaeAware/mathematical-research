Recovering hidden boundaries from accurate predictions

  • Geometry
  • Probability
  • Written coefficient stability, convex moment reconstruction and sample-recovery bounds for fixed complexity and separated, bounded boundaries.

A classifier can predict the right labels without making its underlying rule easy to inspect. This project asks when accurate predictions also determine the geometry: a collection of straight boundaries, with the label changing whenever a point crosses one of them.

For Gaussian observations and a fixed number of separated boundaries, sufficiently small prediction error bounds the error in the polynomial coefficients by a constant times the prediction error. That constant is independent of the surrounding dimension. The results also give convex reconstruction from label measurements and recovery guarantees for corrupted samples.

A reconstruction example

Low-degree label moments summarize how the labels correlate with polynomial features.

In a quadratic example with true boundaries at −1-1−1 and 111, directly treating the label moments as polynomial coefficients puts the boundaries near ±1.32477\pm1.32477±1.32477 and misclassifies about 13.2% of the Gaussian population. Convex reconstruction recovers the target from the same exact moments. In this symmetric one-dimensional example, knowing in advance that the boundaries are at −a-a−a and aaa also lets one recover aaa from the mean label alone.

The coefficient guarantee

Let PPP be a normalized product of rrr distinct affine linear factors, with threshold magnitudes at most TTT and separation at least η\etaη. For a normalized polynomial QQQ of degree at most rrr, sufficiently small Gaussian sign error ε\varepsilonε implies

∥Q−P∥2≤C(r,T,η) ε.\|Q-P\|_2\le C(r,T,\eta)\,\varepsilon.∥Q−P∥2​≤C(r,T,η)ε.

The norm is Gaussian L2L^2L2, equivalently the Euclidean distance between coefficients in an orthonormal Hermite basis. A predictor may use extra coordinates and need not already have the correct factorized form. The constant is uniform in the ambient dimension when rrr, TTT and η\etaη are fixed.

From measurements to boundaries

Direct fitting and moment measurements

The manuscript also compares recovering from moments with fitting the observations directly. If mmm of NNN observations are replaced, an empirical sign-error minimizer satisfies a coefficient bound of order

Or,T,η ⁣(mN+Mlog⁡N+log⁡(1/β)N),M=(n+rr),O_{r,T,\eta}\!\left(\frac{m}{N}+\frac{M\log N+\log(1/\beta)}{N}\right),\qquad M=\binom{n+r}{r},Or,T,η​(Nm​+NMlogN+log(1/β)​),M=(rn+r​),

with probability at least 1−β1-\beta1−β, in the small-error regime. Here nnn is the input dimension.

A two-boundary example gives a raw-sample lower bound of order (m+1)/N(m+1)/N(m+1)/N. A separate conditioning calculation quantifies how moment inversion becomes less stable as two boundaries approach one another. Together these results distinguish the cost of limited measurements from the cost of noisy observations.

Proof and related work

Near a true boundary, the label switches sides. The proof turns the probability of missed sign changes into coefficient estimates, then separates the relevant coordinates from the surrounding ones. Convex reconstruction uses the curvature of a Gaussian absolute-value objective.
