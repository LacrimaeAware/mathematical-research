Choose a finite set of positive integers SSS. Form all products of exactly kkk members of SSS, allowing repetition, and count the distinct answers. Although products collide in complicated ways, the count eventually agrees exactly with a polynomial in kkk.

The question is how long it takes to reach that polynomial regime. This is the arithmetic stabilization question raised in Section 6 of Limbach, Scheidweiler and Triesch, Effective Khovanskii, Ehrhart Polytopes, and the Erdős Multiplication Table Problem.

Why arithmetic changes the bound

Writing each number as its vector of prime exponents turns multiplication into addition. Equal products become integer relations between these vectors. The proof localizes the arithmetic structure of minimal relations and bounds their size; carefully chosen chains supply the lower constructions.

The conversion from relation bounds to eventual polynomial behavior uses established tools, including Granville, Smith and Walker's sumset stabilization theorem and Hilbert-series arguments. The additional step here is the sharp control of the relations arising from bounded integers and rationals.
