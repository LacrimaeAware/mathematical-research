When does a probability law barely change under convolution?

  • Algebra
  • Probability
  • Optimization
  • Written stability bounds for regular probability measures on locally compact Hausdorff groups and for positive states on finite Kac quantum groups, in the stated small-defect ranges.

Choose two independent even hours on a 12-hour clock, uniformly from 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Add them modulo 12. The sum is again uniform on the six even hours. A probability law with this property is called idempotent under convolution.

On a locally compact group, an exactly idempotent probability law is uniform on a compact subgroup, in the sense of Haar probability. How close must a law be to one of these exact examples if combining two independent draws changes the distribution only slightly?

Distance to a subgroup law

Let μ\muμ be a regular probability law on a locally compact Hausdorff group. Write η\etaη for the total variation distance between μ\muμ and the law of the product of two independent draws from μ\muμ. Total variation is the largest difference in the probability assigned to the same event.

For η≤2−39\eta\le 2^{-39}η≤2−39 there is a compact subgroup HHH whose Haar probability uHu_HuH​ satisfies

Finite quantum groups

A related question concerns a state on a finite Kac quantum group. Here convolution combines states through the coproduct of the algebra. For sufficiently small η=∥ϕ∗ϕ−ϕ∥\eta=\|\phi*\phi-\phi\|η=∥ϕ∗ϕ−ϕ∥, there is an exactly idempotent state ψ\psiψ on the same algebra with ∥ϕ−ψ∥≤6η\|\phi-\psi\|\le 6\eta∥ϕ−ψ∥≤6η. Both distances use the norm of linear functionals on that algebra. The bound and the small-defect threshold are uniform in its dimension.

Franz and Skalski describe the structure of exact idempotent states on finite quantum groups. The stability bound concerns the distance to such a state when idempotence holds only approximately. Franz and Skalski, On idempotent states on quantum groups
