Much of my research starts with attempts at famous conjectures. I test the assumptions behind an approach, then look for smaller questions where the same ideas might work. I’m interested in which relationships stay fixed as a problem changes, whether they have practical uses, and what they suggest I should work on next.

## A few questions behind the work

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| How long can a stretch contain no candidate for a fixed prime pattern? | A growing logarithmic gain over the basic gap construction, with substantial Lean formalization through the final theorem. [Prime patterns →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/notes/prime-patterns.html) |
| Can a number fail to fit into bounded factors while its square and cube fit? | An infinite family of obstructions, persisting at every sufficiently large factor limit. [Factorization →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/notes/factor-packing.html) |
| How large can a set be if every three-element choice has a different product? | An upper bound toward the triple-product case of Erdős problem 425. [Distinct products →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/notes/distinct-products.html) |
| When do accurate predictions reveal the boundaries behind them? | Quantitative recovery guarantees, including matching through cancellations and recovery with unknown label noise. [Geometry →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/?subject=geometry#research) |
| What does one repeated observation reveal about hidden stages? | Sharp response bounds from pooled completion times, and recovery from matched repeats. [Hidden stages →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/notes/hidden-stages.html) |

**[Browse all eleven topics →](https://lacrimaeaware.github.io/mathematical-investigations/#research)** Each page introduces the question, connects it to existing work, and explains the result before the approach. Further topics include symmetric networks, recurring prime gaps and arithmetic tensors.
